webscale-edge-lab

The same load-balancing situations run through real HAProxy and Envoy processes in front of small Go backends that can crash, return errors, slow down, fail their health check or be replaced. Everything below is recorded by cmd/record from real client requests on one machine. Personal learning project: loopback only, no VIPs, no real network, no production traffic.

What clients saw

A backend crashes at 1.5 s and returns at 4 s

Rolling deploy: all three backends replaced one by one under load

A backend returns 503 while its health check says healthy

Active health checks only call /healthz, so they never notice. Plain HAProxy keeps sending it a third of the traffic. HAProxy with observe layer7 and Envoy's outlier detection both eject it, then let it back after a pause and have to see errors again before ejecting once more, which is why neither reaches zero.

Who moves when a backend is removed (300 users, header hash)

Things that bit me

Envoy: max_ejection_percent defaults to 10%. With 3 backends that means none may be ejected. Outlier detection detected 33 consecutive-5xx failures and enforced 0 (ejections_overflow counted them). I set it to 50.
Envoy: health checks ran far less often than configured. The admin stats showed 3 successful checks in 4 seconds against a 0.3 s interval. I believe no_traffic_interval (default 60 s) was applying because the proxy-to-backend connections were kept alive and no new connections were opened. A crashed backend was not marked unhealthy and stayed in rotation, hidden by connect-failure retries. Setting no_traffic_interval to the check interval fixed it (check counts went to 42 in the same window).
HAProxy: no passive health checking by default. A backend with working health checks and failing requests keeps its share of traffic until observe layer7 is added.
Overload is handled differently. HAProxy queues requests for a free slot and answers 503 after the queue timeout (about 300 ms here). Envoy's circuit breaker answers 503 within a few milliseconds.
Envoy's ring hash is not perfectly stable. Removing a backend rebuilds the ring over the remaining hosts, so about 19% of users who were not on the removed backend moved, against 0% for HAProxy's consistent hash and about 49% for HAProxy's default map-based hash.
